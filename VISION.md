# Vision

But Why helps people responsible for software work with coding agents direct real software complexity without surrendering judgment over what becomes accepted code.
Its purpose is to connect human intent, agent-assisted implementation, independent validation, and delivery into a trustworthy path from an intended outcome to accepted code.
It owns one thing: making that path understandable, recoverable, and worthy of trust.

## Human responsibility and trust

People remain responsible for system outcomes and consequential decisions.
But Why accepts progress only when a human has made the required outcome and authority to advance it clear.
Human authority covers both the required outcome and the consequential design choices that shape the system that delivers it.
Agents may investigate, propose, and implement, but an agent's confidence or compliance with a workflow does not become durable product authority.
Material implementation constraints and design choices must be made explicit, discussed, or independently judged at the level where they matter, and their uncertainty and evidence remain visible enough for people to understand why work advanced or stopped.
Only choices with no material effect on the system, its supported behavior, or the evidence needed to judge it remain local to implementation.
Acceptance is independent from implementation and proportionate to the risk, uncertainty, and cost of the work.
Evidence is valuable when it can distinguish a meaningful failure from a correct result at a justified creation and maintenance cost.
More tests, broader checks, and more durable records are not valuable merely because there are more of them.
When evidence cannot support a trustworthy judgment, But Why makes the gap visible instead of inventing a requirement or certainty.
Human consent remains required until a class of low-risk work has shown repeated reliable implementation and judgment, while novel or high-risk work keeps explicit human consent.
Repeatable day-to-day mechanics may be automated when they are reliable, while the responsible operator remains accountable for system outcomes and consequential decisions.
When But Why executes agent-authored code, it should protect the operator's environment from that code.
Nonblocking recommendations are useful only when an accountable triage path can retain, judge, and promote them without confusing them with acceptance.
A later rule or preference does not retroactively erase a judgment that was valid for the code and authority it used.

## One connected path with optional entry stages

But Why provides a connected whole of planning, intent, implementation, validation, and delivery rather than a mandatory sequence.
Earlier stages can be skipped when they are not needed, while the remaining stages still make their required context and evidence explicit.
Planning, Tasks, and Task Reviews are optional entry stages.
When a Plan is used, it makes a bounded larger outcome, its consequential decisions, and proposed Task relationships easier to discuss and judge.
A Plan does not silently claim complete scope or turn a proposed mechanism into approved work.
A user may begin with a Change and use validation and Submission without a Task.
Skipping a Task does not remove the need for a clear outcome or operator authority to advance the work.
When Tasks are used, each Task names one coherent, observable, independently assessable part of an approved outcome.
Task authoring preserves the complete approved outcome while dividing work into independently understandable, implementable, assessable, and verifiable units.
A split is appropriate only when separate parts need materially independent implementation, failure handling, verification reasoning, or design decisions.
Work stays together when a split would leave an unsafe, unsupported, duplicated, or unjudgeable result.
Decomposition should not merely distribute files, technical layers, or agent effort without creating independently trustworthy progress.
A separate Interactive Session for an Implementer is optional, and people may work directly in the Change's Managed Worktree when that suits the work.
The connected path should make these choices explicit rather than creating hidden alternate workflows.

## Deterministic orchestration around agent work

Agent work is useful and nondeterministic.
But Why should provide deterministic, programmable orchestration around that work so repeatable workflow mechanics do not depend on agents faithfully following Markdown instructions.
The product should carry durable sequencing, preconditions, evidence capture, and recovery responsibilities that can be made mechanical.
This orchestration should leave agents room to reason and adapt within their role without making them responsible for enforcing the system's lifecycle or authority rules.

## Local first, replaceable, and extensible

But Why should be useful in a Local Repository without requiring a remote service at any point.
Remote backends and Candidate Publication remain opt-in and configurable for users who need remote collaboration or delivery.
Delivery through another code-review host should preserve the same trust boundaries when users need it.
Storage, coordination, delivery hosts, and agent execution are replaceable means rather than the product's purpose.
But Why does not commit in advance to one future mechanism or require interchangeable implementations of each current mechanism.
Possible future substitutions include, for example, repository-hosted records instead of local state, another code-review host, remote coordination, or another local or cloud agent executor.
The current mechanism should remain inside the boundary that owns its concern, and its incidental representation should not become part of the product contract.
That containment must not become a speculative provider interface, runtime interchangeability requirement, or generalized abstraction before a concrete supported alternative establishes the shared contract.
When a concrete need makes variation valuable, the applicable capability should become configurable or pluggable at the owner that can preserve its authority, lifecycle, failures, and evidence.
When users choose remote delivery or cross-repository outcomes, But Why should preserve the outcome and each contributing Change's code identity, judgments, completion facts, and recovery boundaries.
A change to those means is worthwhile only when it keeps the path from human intent to accepted code understandable and recoverable.
When recurring misses appear, people should be able to improve the responsible review layer by extending or replacing reviewer instructions or Specialist Reviewer roles.
Such improvement should preserve clear ownership, human authority, and legible evidence rather than add ceremony or silently broaden accepted intent.

## Product boundary

But Why is not a generic project tracker, pipeline engine, agent runtime, repository host, or supply-chain attestation product.
It should make primary evidence legible before adding another presentation for the same judgment.
These principles are the lasting vision, while architecture and context documentation define the current supported system and its canonical relationships.
A product change aligns when it helps people shape consequential complexity into independently judged progress without weakening human authority or the meaning of accepted evidence.
A product change should be resisted when it hides a consequential decision, obscures authority or the code under judgment, or adds machinery that cannot improve a trustworthy decision.
